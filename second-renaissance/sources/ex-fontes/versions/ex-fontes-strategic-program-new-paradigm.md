# **Ex Fontes – Science for Life: A Strategic Program for a New Paradigm**

## **Part 1: The Philosophical Bedrock: Synthesizing Wisdom for a New Paradigm**

### **1.1. Identifying the Core Intellectual Thread: The "Illusion of Separation" and the Call for an Integrative Worldview**

The East-West Circle (EWC) articulates a profound ambition: "To be globally recognized as free thinkers, who lead initiatives for a better world by blending Eastern and Western wisdom traditions, science for life and lessons from personal growth programmes".1 This ambition finds a deep resonance and intellectual underpinning in the critical analysis presented in documents such as "Ex Fontes – Science for Life V7," which posits that "the dominant Western worldview has been shaped by a deeply rooted sense of separation: mind from body, spirituality from matter, the self from nature, and the individual from the collective".1 This "Illusion of Separation" is not a recent phenomenon but a legacy that has evolved over millennia, shaping cultures, philosophies, and societal structures.1

The stated purpose of the East-West Circle is "To motivate people to take an interest in ancient wisdom from the East and the West as a stepping stone to find innovative ways to address major challenges of the world we live in".1 This notion of a "stepping stone" directly aligns with the call to action in "Ex Fontes – Science for Life V7," which suggests that "once we recognize that a worldview is a lens, not the landscape, we begin to reclaim our freedom... to consciously imagine new possibilities and co-create a more expansive and meaningful story for humanity".1

Thus, the East-West Circle is positioned not merely as a forum for intellectual discourse but as an active catalyst for addressing the fundamental drivers of contemporary global crises. It seeks to achieve this by directly challenging the prevailing paradigm of separation. The "blending" of diverse knowledge systems—Eastern and Western, ancient and modern, scientific and wisdom-based—is not intended as a superficial amalgamation. Instead, it represents a concerted effort towards a deep synthesis, aiming to cultivate a new, integrated worldview capable of navigating the complexities of the 21st century. The EWC's core principles—"East Meets West, Modern Meets Ancient, Science Meets Wisdom" 1—underscore a commitment to holistic engagement. This holistic approach is further reflected in the vision for a "Second Renaissance" and a "Second Enlightenment," which emphasize a "reconnection with Nature" and the integration of "the feeling heart" alongside rational inquiry.1 For the EWC, and particularly its "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" initiative, to effectively counteract the deeply ingrained "Illusion of Separation" and foster this integrative worldview, its programmatic offerings must extend beyond intellectual dissemination. They must incorporate immersive and participatory formats that allow individuals to experientially access and embody this sense of interconnectedness.

### **1.2. The "Metacrisis" as Context: Why "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" is a Critical Response**

The contemporary global landscape is increasingly understood through the lens of a "metacrisis." This term, as explored in "Synthesizing Metacrisis Thinkers' Insights Final," signifies "a confluence of deeply interconnected crises... a fundamental crisis of our underlying systems of meaning-making, governance, and collective operation".1 The analysis further suggests that "addressing the metacrisis demands more than superficial policy adjustments or technological fixes; it necessitates a profound transformation in our collective consciousness".1 This perspective is strongly echoed in "Ex Fontes – Science for Life V7," which describes a "polycrisis" rooted in the "Illusion of Separation" and exacerbated by economic models like Neoliberal Capitalism. This polycrisis manifests across ecological, social, health, geopolitical, and spiritual domains.1

Against this backdrop, the East-West Circle's vision—that "Guidance and insights from ancient wisdom from different traditions are most relevant for personal and spiritual growth as well as finding practical solutions to modern days challenges" 1—becomes particularly salient. The "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" initiative, one of the EWC's three core pillars 1, is strategically positioned as a direct and critical response to this metacrisis. Its aim is not merely to apply existing scientific knowledge to life's problems in a conventional sense. Rather, it seeks to contribute to the transformation of the very "systems of meaning-making" and "collective consciousness" that lie at the heart of the metacrisis. The initiative is designed to demonstrate "how insights from ancient wisdom can underpin the design of new technological solutions and modern lifestyle practices for a better world".\[1, 1\] This implies the co-creation of a *new science of life*—one that is inherently life-affirming, integrates profound wisdom, and directly addresses the root causes of the metacrisis by fostering an evolution in human consciousness and our frameworks for understanding reality.

While the philosophical ambition for "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" as a "fusion of ancient insights and modern discovery" and a "reunion of science and wisdom" is clearly articulated 1, and the urgency of the metacrisis provides a compelling "why" 1, the specific programmatic mechanisms to achieve this fusion remain to be fully elaborated. The current documentation outlines the *what* and the *why*, but the detailed *how*—the methodologies, research questions, collaborative structures, and tangible outputs through which "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" will realize its transformative potential and directly engage with the concerns raised by metacrisis thinkers—represents a key area for development. The program proposed herein aims to address this specific need, providing a clear pathway for this vital initiative.

## **Part 2: Current Landscape and Strategic Opportunity for "Ex Fontes – Science for Life"**

### **2.1. Analysis of the East-West Circle's Existing Framework and Strengths**

The East-West Circle is not embarking on the "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" initiative from a vacuum. It possesses a robust foundational structure, a compelling narrative deeply rooted in historical precedents, and a diverse portfolio of engagement mechanisms that provide a strong launchpad for this ambitious program.

The EWC's vision "To be globally recognized as free thinkers..." and its purpose "To motivate people to take an interest in ancient wisdom..." are clearly articulated, providing a guiding light for its activities.1 Its operational principles—"East Meets West, Modern Meets Ancient, Science Meets Wisdom"—further define its unique integrative approach.1 A significant source of inspiration and structural analogy is the Platonic Academy of Florence.\[1, 1\] This historical connection lends intellectual weight and a tested model for engaging different levels of supporters, mirrored in the EWC's structure of Noble Patrons (akin to the Academy's *Genus Heroicum*), the Emerald Circle of 100 (reflecting the *Consuetudine Familiars*), and the broader community of Enthusiasts (paralleling the *Auditors*).\[1, 1\]

The EWC's activities are organized under three distinct pillars: "Ad Fontes" (focused on making ancient texts accessible), "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" (the focus of this report, aiming to show how innovators use ancient wisdom for new solutions), and "Ex Fontes – Transformative Programmes" (delivering programs for personal and spiritual growth).1 Several programs and activities are already operational, creating existing channels for engagement and revenue. These include the prestigious Emerald Circle of 100, with its 12-month "The Key" initiation journey designed to immerse participants in diverse wisdom traditions and contemporary challenges.\[1, 1, 1, 1\] The Circle Community offers a more accessible entry point through a €555 annual donation, providing benefits such as access to the East-West Room, the community app, and "Goodevening free thinker" online sessions.\[1, 1, 1, 1\] The Ficino Series offers deep dives into specific wisdom traditions \[1, 1, 1, 1\], while Experience Labs provide interactive explorations of ancient wisdom and modern innovation.\[1, 1, 1\] Furthermore, annual Banquets—the Cosimo de’ Medici Banquet on September 27th and the Jacob Böhme Banquet on April 24th—serve as key events for engaging Noble Patrons.\[1, 1, 1\]

A significant asset is the EWC's physical hub, the East-West Room, located within the historic Embassy of the Free Mind in Amsterdam.1 This provides a prestigious and symbolic home for its activities. The financial model aims for self-sustainability through a combination of income from these various programs and patron support, with budget projections outlined for 2025 and 2026\.1 A defined core team with specific roles (Jozef Ritman, Annette Nijs, Mathew Dawkins, Dan Vercammen, Angela Verkade, and others are mentioned in relation to governance and leadership 1) and a clear collaboration model for working with third parties \[1, 1\] further solidify its operational capacity.

This existing infrastructure—a recognized brand associated with the Embassy of the Free Mind, a physical center, established leadership, multiple income streams, and proven program formats—means that the "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" initiative can leverage these assets rather than needing to develop everything from the ground up. However, the current financial projections (e.g., a 2026 income target of €300,000 \- €325,000 1) and team structure, while ambitious, may require augmentation to fully realize the potential of a significantly expanded "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" program, especially one involving sustained engagement with high-profile global changemakers and a major international event in 2026\. The design of the proposed program will therefore need to consider resource implications carefully, potentially identifying needs for new funding avenues or strategic partnerships specifically dedicated to this flagship stream.

The East-West Circle, with its profound vision to blend Eastern and Western wisdom, ancient insights, and modern science, stands at a pivotal moment. The "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" initiative, as outlined in this strategic program, offers a compelling pathway to address the deep-seated "Illusion of Separation" that underpins many of our contemporary global crises—the metacrisis. By convening leading global changemakers and fostering a rigorous, co-creative dialogue over a two-year period, this program aims to articulate and disseminate an integrated "Science for Life" capable of inspiring a "Second Renaissance."

**Key Conclusions:**

1. **The Core Intellectual Thread is Sound and Urgent:** The EWC's focus on overcoming the "Illusion of Separation" by synthesizing diverse knowledge forms directly addresses the root causes of the metacrisis, as identified by numerous leading thinkers.\[1, 1\] This provides a powerful and relevant intellectual foundation.  
2. **"Ex Fontes – Science for Life" is the Key Strategic Initiative:** While all three EWC pillars are important, "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" holds the potential to be the flagship initiative that most directly engages with global challenges and positions the EWC as a significant voice in shaping a new paradigm. The planned "Second Renaissance: The Return of the Free Thinker" event in September 2026 provides a clear focal point for this ambition.1  
3. **A Cohesive Programmatic Thrust is Essential:** The primary "lacking" identified was the absence of a detailed, multi-year strategy to translate the profound vision for "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" into tangible action and sustained engagement. The proposed rhythmic monthly program and the detailed structure for the 2026 event aim to fill this gap.  
4. **Leveraging Existing Assets is Crucial for Success:** The EWC's established infrastructure, including its brand, physical hub, existing program formats, and community engagement channels, provides a strong foundation upon which "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" can build efficiently and effectively.1  
5. **Co-Creation with Changemakers is Key:** The engagement of global changemakers must be genuinely co-creative, inviting them not just as speakers but as active partners in shaping the intellectual outputs and the direction of the initiative. This makes the journey itself a valuable outcome.

**Strategic Recommendations:**

1. **Prioritize and Resource "Ex Fontes – Science for Life":** Given its potential impact and alignment with the 2026 "Second Renaissance" event, the EWC leadership should formally adopt and prioritize the "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" program as a central strategic focus. This includes allocating or securing dedicated resources (financial and human) for its successful implementation, potentially forming a dedicated steering committee or working group under the leadership of Michael Dawkins.1  
2. **Implement the Rhythmic Monthly Program:** The detailed monthly program blueprint (Table 2\) should be adopted as the core operational plan for the 2024-2026 period. This provides the necessary structure for thematic exploration, changemaker engagement, content development, and community building leading to the 2026 event.  
3. **Actively Cultivate Changemaker and Patron Relationships:** Initiate personalized outreach to the identified global changemakers (including those newly researched) immediately, emphasizing the co-creative nature of their involvement. Simultaneously, develop a targeted strategy to engage existing and new Noble Patrons specifically around the vision and impact of "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" and the "Second Renaissance" event.  
4. **Embrace Participatory and Experiential Methodologies:** Ensure that program activities, particularly those involving changemakers and the Emerald Circle, incorporate participatory methodologies (e.g., Warm Data Labs, Theory U processes 1) and experiential elements to foster deep dialogue, genuine synthesis, and embodied understanding, moving beyond purely intellectual engagement.  
5. **Develop a Comprehensive Communication Strategy:** Create a clear and compelling communication plan that articulates the vision of "Ex Fontes – Science for Life," the journey towards the 2026 "Second Renaissance," and how various EWC activities contribute to this overarching narrative. This will be crucial for engaging the wider community, attracting partners, and building global momentum.  
6. **Plan for Legacy and Sustained Impact:** From the outset, design the "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" program with a view to its long-term evolution beyond 2026\. This includes planning for the dissemination of knowledge outputs, the nurturing of the community, and the integration of the initiative's work into the EWC's 5-year strategic plan and future funding mechanisms.1

The journey towards a "Second Renaissance" is ambitious, but the East-West Circle, through a focused and strategically executed "Ex Fontes – Science for Life" program, is uniquely positioned to make a significant contribution. By fostering a genuine "reunion of science and wisdom" 1, it can indeed inspire and equip a new generation of "free thinkers" to co-create a more conscious, integrated, and regenerative future for all.
